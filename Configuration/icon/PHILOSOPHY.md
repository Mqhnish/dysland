# Field Lines

A dark body attracts light. Everything in the image follows from that one force.

Form comes from attraction. Dozens of fine lines cross the field at rest, then bend toward a single point, the way iron filings show a magnet nobody can see. No line is drawn for its own sake. Each is a calculated path, and together they make a landmass that was never outlined.

Colour is heat. It is brightest where the lines crowd together and cools as they spread apart, moving from pale gold through ember into deep blue dusk. There are no flat fills and no decorative gradients, only light adding up where paths overlap.

Scale has to hold at both ends. Up close, the work rewards patient looking: every strand is separate, and spacing tightens by exact degrees. At the size of a fingernail, the same strands fuse into one clear silhouette. The composition is meticulously resolved for both distances, the product of painstaking calibration and not of a lucky render.

The composition is nearly still. One dark shape sits on the axis, one mass rises to meet it, and a narrow gap holds them apart. The tension lives in that gap, and master-level restraint means adding nothing else. No letters, no ornament, no second idea.

Craft here is invisible. Edges are exact, curves are continuous, and the blacks are tuned so that darkness still has depth. It should look as though someone spent countless hours removing things until only the force remained.
